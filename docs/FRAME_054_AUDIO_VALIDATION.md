# FRAME 0.5.4 audio reliability fix, 2026-10-13

R (user, real use with Desktop audio + Microphone): Shadow Recording stopped twice within a minute (2026-10-13 20:18:11 and 20:19:08 local): "Audio · An audio device stopped or became unavailable" with "Audio output stalled for more than one second." resp. "Audio device interrupted", plus FFmpeg's "Guessed Channel Layout: stereo". The user's `%LOCALAPPDATA%\FRAME\diagnostics.log` (read only) shows the same two faults already in 0.5.3 (15:34, 15:38 UTC) and 0.5.4 (18:07, 18:08, 18:18, 18:19 UTC): not a 0.5.4 or separation regression.

## Root cause (reproduced, no audio device involved)
Audio reaches FFmpeg as a continuous PCM stream through a named pipe written by `AudioPipe` (20 ms chunks paced by the wall clock); video comes from `gfxcapture`, which delivers no frames while the captured content is unchanged. FFmpeg's scheduler throttles any input that runs ahead of the slowest output stream, so **while the screen is still, FFmpeg stops reading the audio pipe**. The old pump then blocked in `WriteAsync`; the WASAPI rings (1 s) overflowed in the capture thread. When the screen changed again, the pump either finished its write > 1 s late → "Audio output stalled for more than one second", or saw the overflow → "Audio device interrupted" → `CaptureCoordinator.Fail` → Shadow Recording stopped (auto-restart, max 3/h). No device was lost and silence was not the trigger (silence was already written as zeros).
Evidence (`--audio-test`, real Windows Graphics Capture of a test window, synthetic audio source — no Windows audio device opened):
- Unfixed code: during a 12 s still window the pipe accepted nothing (chunks written stuck at 240 for 12 s, video frames stuck at 131–132; one write blocked 5.5 s); on resume the identical fault text appeared: "Audio output stalled for more than one second. [aist#0:0/pcm_f32le] Guessed Channel Layout: stereo".
- A comparison build with the old pump (scratch copy) also faulted with the same message in one of two short runs with continuous motion: short throttling is enough.
Secondary problems found and fixed on the way:
- A full WASAPI ring threw (stopping recording) instead of dropping; over hours, device-clock drift alone could fill the 1 s ring. A `DATA_DISCONTINUITY` flag (a glitch report, e.g. loopback resuming) also stopped recording.
- Once audio survived still periods: after a still longer than ~10 s FFmpeg fills the gap with duplicated video frames in one burst while the audio for that time follows from the spool; the muxer only waited 10 s (`max_interleave_delta`) for audio, wrote video alone, and the late audio landed in later segments with wrong times — replays spanning the still period showed a frozen picture to the end (test B, variants). Without audio this does not happen.
- A planned stop could race with FFmpeg closing the audio input (counted as a failure).

## Fix (`AudioPipe.cs`, `Backend.cs`, Core `AudioHealth.cs`, `AudioStatusView.cs`)
- **Producer/writer split.** The producer (wall clock, 20 ms) drains every source and never waits for FFmpeg; the writer feeds the pipe from `AudioSpool` and may wait as long as FFmpeg does not read. Up to 30 s of real audio is kept meanwhile (≈12 MB worst case); older audio becomes "owed silence" in place, so the number of samples, i.e. the audio timeline and A/V sync, stays exact while memory stays bounded. No 1-second stall rule any more.
- **Genuine failure only:** `AudioHealth.InputBroken` = writer blocked ≥ 10 s while the audio already accepted is ≥ 5 s behind the video (FFmpeg not consuming audio although it should) → fault "The capture engine stopped accepting audio." (restart as before). A broken pipe (FFmpeg closed the input) is also a failure, except after `Stopping()`.
- **Source degradation and recovery:** a source that fails or cannot start (device removed, `AUDCLNT_E_DEVICE_INVALIDATED`, audio service restart, privacy block, no device) is replaced by silence and restarted with backoff 0.5/1/2/5/10 s; video and the FFmpeg process keep running; no encoder restart. `IMMNotificationClient` (event-driven) switches sources that follow the Windows default when the default changes (start new, then swap: no gap) and retries at once when devices appear or change state.
- **WASAPI:** full ring drops the oldest frame (counted), discontinuity is counted, not fatal. Capture format/resampling unchanged.
- **FFmpeg:** `-ch_layout stereo` instead of `-ac 2` (same stream; removes the "Guessed Channel Layout" warning); with audio, `-max_interleave_delta 0` so the muxer waits for audio after a burst (FFmpeg's scheduler already keeps video from running ahead otherwise; the queue only grows during a post-still burst).
- **User feedback:** top-right "Audio interrupted · Recording continues without audio" (or "Desktop audio interrupted" / "Microphone interrupted" when the other source still works) after an outage lasting ≥ 1.5 s, "Audio restored · Recording with audio again" only after such a note, at most 3 interruption notes per 10 min (Core `AudioNoticePolicy`); brief switches are silent. Capture-page status line names the source and reason; tray tooltip "… active – audio interrupted"; `diagnostics.log` gets `audio-interrupted` / `audio-restored`. Stops still use the existing "Shadow Recording stopped · Open FRAME for details" and panel.
- **Defaults (user decision 2026-10-13):** Desktop audio on for new installations; Microphone stays opt-in; saved choices are kept (settings files contain every key). Both use the Windows default devices unless a device was chosen in Settings.
- Performance: same 20 ms producer cadence and mixing cost as before, one writer task waiting on a semaphore, notifications event-driven, no polling, no extra FFmpeg process, encoder settings unchanged.

## Pre-existing finding (not changed): absolute A/V offset at start
Audio is ~0.43–0.61 s **late** relative to video in every session, measured with flash + sample-exact beep: audio time 0 is when FFmpeg opens the audio pipe, video time 0 is its first frame (`setpts=PTS-STARTPTS`), which comes ~0.5 s later (gfxcapture/encoder start). The old pump shows the same (0.57–0.59 s in the comparison build), the new one 0.44–0.61 s. Still periods and recovery do not change it (Δ ≤ 0.04 s). Fixing it means aligning the two start times (e.g. a shared clock origin for audio and video); that touches FFmpeg timing and needs its own decision and validation.

## Limitations
- Very long still periods with audio: after the still, FFmpeg encodes the duplicated gap in a burst (pre-existing) and the muxer now holds those packets until the audio arrives (seconds); for stills of many minutes this is a transient memory peak (estimated tens to hundreds of MB for an hour at high resolution; not measured). Audio older than 30 s within one still period is replaced by silence.
- Real device removal/re-plug and real Windows default switching were not performed (tests must not change the user's devices); they are simulated through the same code paths (failures with real HRESULTs, the notification flag). Physical check by hand recommended.
- Multiple displays as independent pipelines open one audio pipe each (pre-existing).
